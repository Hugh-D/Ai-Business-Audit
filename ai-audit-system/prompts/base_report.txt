## Diagnostic Scoring Framework
For each relevant problem area, classify the current state as green, yellow, or red.

Green means structured systems exist, ownership is clear, visibility and measurement are present, and execution is consistent.
Yellow means the process is partially structured, depends on certain people, has some visibility gaps, or is inconsistently executed.
Red means the process is reactive, heavily owner-dependent, manually handled, poorly tracked, or inconsistent.

Assess these areas when there is transcript evidence:
- Lead Response: after-hours handling, missed calls, callback speed, text-back, answering service, call routing.
- Lead Follow-Up: speed, consistency, ownership, reminders, and whether missed enquiries are recovered.
- Lead Ownership: whether one person or role clearly owns lead follow-up.
- Lead Visibility: whether the business knows how many leads go cold or slip through the cracks.
- Quote Follow-Up: what happens after a quote or estimate is sent.
- Quote Conversion Tracking: whether quote win rates or conversion patterns are measured.
- Quote Delays: what delays quoting and whether turnaround is predictable.
- Job Profitability: whether the business can see which jobs are profitable.
- Admin Overload: repetitive admin, manual chasing, and staff overload.
- Double Handling: re-entering information across multiple systems.
- Workflow Visibility: whether active jobs can be seen in one place.
- Scheduling: rescheduling, reactive planning, downtime, and dispatch or route friction.
- Timesheets: whether time tracking is digital, consistent, or manually chased.
- Material Ordering: supplier, inventory, or last-minute ordering delays.
- Rework and Errors: missed communication, unclear handoffs, and mistakes that create rework.
- Owner Dependence: approvals, quoting, escalation, scheduling, or knowledge held by the owner.
- Operational Dependency: what would break if the owner stepped away for two weeks.
- Team Alignment: how the team stays aligned day to day.
- Hiring: whether recruiting is repeatable or reactive.
- Onboarding: whether training is structured or mostly shadowing.
- Staff Retention: frustration, burnout, confusion, or retention risk.
- Customer Updates: whether customers get proactive updates or have to chase.
- Reviews and Reputation: whether reviews or referrals are requested consistently.
- Customer Friction: complaints, delays, unclear expectations, or communication gaps.
- Tool Fragmentation: whether systems work together or require manual copying.
- Automation Readiness: openness to automation, prior attempts, and implementation confidence.
- Repetitive Work: forgotten, repeated, or manually chased tasks.
- Strategic Priority: the one issue that would matter most in the next 90 days.

Priority weighting:
- Highest weight: lead handling, quote follow-up, owner dependence, and customer communication. These usually have the fastest ROI, clearest business impact, and easiest implementation wins.
- Medium weight: admin duplication, workflow visibility, scheduling, and reviews.
- Lower initial weight: culture, hiring maturity, and long-term organizational systems. These are still important, but are usually harder to solve quickly in an early implementation engagement.

For each diagnostic finding, identify:
- what is wrong or healthy
- what it is likely costing the business
- how urgent it is
- how easy it is to improve
- what would create the fastest visible win

Only include diagnostic findings supported by the transcript. If evidence is missing, say so in the relevant section rather than inventing details.
