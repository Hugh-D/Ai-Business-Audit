# Phone Number Blocked Fallback

## Situation

SIPCity has not replied yet, and Twilio compliance is blocking progress.

Do not let phone-number procurement become the bottleneck for validation. The current commercial question is:

> Will electricians value the audit report enough to book a follow-up call or buy an implementation sprint?

That can be tested before the branded inbound number is live.

## Validation Path While Waiting

Use Hugh-led discovery calls as the temporary intake path.

Flow:

1. Prospect agrees to a free electrician revenue leak audit.
2. Hugh conducts the 15 to 20 minute discovery call by normal phone, Zoom, Google Meet, or Teams.
3. Hugh asks for consent to record/transcribe or takes structured notes.
4. The transcript or notes are pasted into the existing app using the manual audit workflow.
5. Hugh reviews the generated report.
6. Report is delivered.
7. Hugh asks whether they want a follow-up call or implementation help.

This is less automated, but it tests the same buyer value: diagnosis, report trust, follow-up demand, and sprint opportunity.

## Temporary Positioning

Use this until the public audit number is ready:

> I am testing a quick revenue leak audit for electrical businesses. For the first few, I am running the discovery call myself, then using the audit system to generate and review the report. It takes about 15 to 20 minutes and I will send you the findings afterwards.

If asked about AI:

> The AI helps structure the assessment and draft the report, but I review the findings before sending them.

Avoid over-explaining the phone-number issue. Prospects do not need that detail.

## Temporary Booking Ask

Use this instead of "call the audit number":

> Great. I can run the audit call with you directly while the public audit number is being set up. It takes about 15 to 20 minutes. Would today or tomorrow suit better?

## Manual Discovery Questions

Keep the call close to the AI voice flow:

1. Can you give me a quick overview of the electrical work you mainly do?
2. How do new enquiries usually come in: phone, website, referrals, Google, or something else?
3. What happens if someone calls after hours or when no one can answer?
4. How are quotes followed up after an inspection, site visit, or enquiry?
5. What software or tools do you currently use to run the business?
6. How do you currently ask happy customers for Google reviews?
7. Where do jobs or leads most often get delayed, forgotten, or handled manually?
8. Do you offer any repeat work, maintenance, safety checks, smoke alarm checks, switchboard upgrades, EV charger work, or reminders?
9. Do you have a website you want included in the assessment?
10. What email should I send the report to?
11. Would you like a follow-up call with Hugh to talk through the findings?

Do not consult during discovery. Capture the workflow first.

## What Counts As A Valid Audit

A fallback audit counts toward the first 10 if:

- The prospect is an electrician or small electrical contractor.
- They opted into the audit.
- Hugh captured enough discovery detail to generate a useful report.
- A reviewed report was sent.
- The outcome was recorded in [electrician-audit-tracker.csv](electrician-audit-tracker.csv).

## Phone Provider Next Steps

Keep telco work narrow:

- Wait for SIPCity response.
- If SIPCity replies positively, continue with SIPCity/Retell setup.
- If SIPCity stalls for another business day, send one concise follow-up asking for the recommended inbound number setup and timeline.
- Do not spend days trialling more phone vendors before at least a few manual audits are completed.
- Do not build new product features to work around the number issue unless manual audits reveal a real sales blocker.

## Stop Rule

If 5 manual electrician audits produce no follow-up calls, no implementation interest, and no repeated pain pattern, pause the phone-number chase and revisit the offer or niche angle.

If manual audits produce interest, then the phone number is operational leverage, not product validation.
